How we built a production SaaS for service businesses

Building a landing page with a login form and building a real multi-tenant SaaS that companies run their daily operations on are two different worlds. The hard parts of SaaS are invisible on screenshots: tenant data isolation, billing, messenger pipelines, and 24/7 uptime. Here is how we designed, shipped, and operate Ladero — our cloud CRM platform.

What a real SaaS needs beyond the UI

The marketing page and forms are 10% of the work. The remaining 90% determines whether the product survives in production:

  • Strict multi-tenancy — every company's data is isolated by tenant_id. A mistake here means cross-company data leaks.
  • Authentication & RBAC — password, email OTP, magic links, and role permissions within each client team.
  • Billing & plan limits — subscription tiers, quota enforcement, payment webhooks, and invoices.
  • Messenger integrations — WhatsApp Business, Telegram, MAX, and per-tenant email in one unified inbox.
  • 24/7 operations — automated deployments, monitoring, backups, and zero-downtime migrations.

Why multi-tenancy must be baked in on day one

In a true SaaS, one codebase and database cluster serve every customer while enforcing strict tenant_id scoping on every query. New customers sign up and start working immediately without spinning up separate instances. Retrofitting multi-tenancy onto a single-tenant prototype almost always requires a full rewrite.

One engine — multiple brands by domain

The same platform powers both Ladero (ladero.ru) and a commercial network of service centers (atkweb.ru) with separate domains, branding, and email routing — zero code duplication.
